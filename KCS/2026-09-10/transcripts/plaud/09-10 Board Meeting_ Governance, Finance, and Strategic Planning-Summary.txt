- Governance Standard Review and Board Governance
  - FCIS Governance Standard 2.4 reviewed: Board governs/formulates policy; Head of School manages staff, implements policy, oversees operations. A governance standard review will be added to each agenda and documented in minutes.
  - Adoption of the “Governance Review and Committee Charter Recommendations” (dated July 20, 2026) as a draft “living document,” with a note to include stewardship liaison committee information.
  - Governance document review areas: Articles Four, Five, Six, Seven; documents affirm biblical foundation, purpose, partnership model, session authority, and Board of Trustees responsibilities.
  - Board composition constraints discussed: 60% trustees must be Kendall Presbyterian Church members; all trustees must be PCA members. Governance Committee to present options to amend Article 4; advisory opinion process to session required.
  - Officer roles misalignment: bylaws list President, Vice President, Treasurer; current practice uses Chair and lacks VP/Treasurer. Consider generalizing role language (e.g., “a board member responsible for finances”) or amend bylaws to reflect current structure.
  - Board meeting frequency: bylaws require monthly meetings; current practice is every six weeks, causing noncompliance. Consensus leaned toward minimum quarterly meetings with flexibility (including online), but no formal change adopted.
- Church Bylaws and Dissolution Protection
  - Discrepancy identified: school constitution protects church if school dissolves; church bylaws lack reciprocal protection for school if church dissolves. Proposed amending Article Seven to protect school assets (e.g., first right of refusal) and exploring incorporation under the church’s umbrella; legal/tax implications (e.g., sales tax exemption) require counsel.
- Special Congregational Meeting for Bylaw Changes
  - Timing discussed: aim for a special meeting before year-end (not wait until January). Requires petition from 25 members, one-week notice, and at least 25 attendees for a vote. Confidence expressed in passing changes with current board composition.

- Fundraising Pledges: Recognition and Spending Policy
  - GAAP approach: recognize pledge revenue at commitment (similar to receivables); uncollectible pledges written off; legal enforceability noted but action discretionary. Donor communications should clarify binding nature and budgeting reliance.

  - Consensus to develop a formal policy: hybrid approach allowing spending up to collected amounts; spending beyond collections requires specific board approval. Consider establishing a bad debt reserve as fundraising grows; request made for cash flow projections, though depth of detail debated.
