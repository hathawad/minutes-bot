**Devotion**

Jerry shared from **Psalm 63**, written by David in the desert of Judah while fleeing from his son Absalom (the period described in **2 Samuel 15-19**). David's situation was one of intense personal and national stress, yet the psalm paints a picture of complete dependence on God. Jerry highlighted three movements in the psalm:

* **Declaration:** *"You God are my God."*
* **Desire:** *"Earnestly I seek you, I thirst for you, my whole being longs for you, in a dry and parched land where there is no water."* The words **seek**, **thirst**, and **long** show the depth of David's pursuit of God.
* **Delight and praise:** Having seen God's power and glory in the sanctuary, David lifts his hands, praises with his lips, and meditates on God through the night watches: *"Because you are my help, I sing in the shadow of your wings. I cling to you. Your right hand upholds me."*

**Application:** Trials are meant to grow our faith, but they are not easy. Sometimes we earnestly seek God; other times we drift, lean on ourselves, science, or other people. Jerry shared the familiar illustration: if you and God are on a phone call and you can't hear Him, guess who moved? It wasn't God. When we repent and return, He is faithful to restore fellowship.

**Challenge to the Board:** Over the coming meetings the Board will be working hard to bring its governance into compliance with FCIS standards. This is necessary and good. But just as David could be distracted, the Board can become so focused on the technical side of accreditation that it loses sight of the spiritual mission. Jerry reminded the Board that the FCIS visiting team explicitly noted *"the spirit of the Lord"* in the school. The Board must continue to walk the talk, not just bear the name "Christian." As we improve how we are structured and function, we must equally consider **how we will plan, how we will implement, and whether it remains aligned with our values, mission, and goals**.

Jerry also noted, addressing Mrs. Donate: her daughter has been at the Wayside campus for two years and will be at KCS for another nine — eleven years of formative input across the developmental years of her life. The Board's stewardship matters at exactly that scale.

Jerry closed with a reminder to keep returning to the **mission statement**.

* **Cost Analysis - Shared Expenses:**

  * **Context:** It has been roughly a year since the **church relaunch**.   * **Kris's Research (industry benchmarks):**
    * In healthy church/school relationships where the school is a ministry of the church and the church covers admin/janitorial/etc., the school typically pays **10-20%** of its budget toward shared costs.
    * KCS is currently paying approximately **9%** in total shared costs (including its monthly contribution), but: KCS keeps tuition below market and **funds its own admin and janitorial** rather than relying on the church for those.
    * Averaged across categories, the current shared-expense split is roughly **82% school / 18% church** — heavy on the school.
    * Common allocation methods: square footage for facilities/utilities/maintenance, headcount for admin/shared staffing, usage hours for classrooms, or actual usage metrics. Alternatives include a facility-use fee (rent model) or a church subsidy model (not applicable at KPC).
