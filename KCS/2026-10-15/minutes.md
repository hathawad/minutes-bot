**7:38pm - Finance Update - Kris Roth**

**Flourish Investment**

* Flourish interest paid for the month was **$4,034**. Year to date the account has earned roughly **$36,000** since January [transcript garbled: "30 and 6000 from January"], or about **$4,000 per month**, which matches the budgeted amount.
* In the past, interest income was not included in the budget. It is now budgeted revenue, so the Board has to be more careful about relying on it.
* Roughly **$1 million** is swept into the account to earn interest. The current rate is **3.44%**.
* Kris noted a nine-month CD rate of **4.25%** [context unclear; see notes], and suggested that research be done on whether a better option is available. Terey clarified that any decision to move the funds would come on recommendation from the Board, not from the business office. It was noted that Georgina set up the account with an advisor's help, and that Jim, as a finance committee member, follows the market.

**Restricted Funds and Re-budget**

* Following the last meeting's discussion of restricted fund accounting for the UKnight Fund, Kris asked Ana and Georgina to separate the restricted funds from the operating budget so each could be seen on its own.
* In doing so, they found that the budget had been built on **488 students**, while actual enrollment came in at **471** [Terey suggested 477; see notes], a revenue shortfall of about **$300,000**.
* To accommodate this when redistributing the budget, the business office removed the **$250,000** of planned transfers to reserves (building fund and capital fund). The operating budget still shows a **$100,000** loss for the year.
* **The finance committee will re-budget and bring a revised budget back to the Board for approval**, because the school cannot have an operating budget with a $100,000 loss in it. Kris did not want projections simply substituted for budget figures; the projections need to be reviewed to see what is changing and whether they are realistic.
* **Current projections:** a small operating surplus of about **$3,000**. At the end of August, the operating budget was **$36,000** in the black. The overall projection was about **$115,000**, because fundraising is expected to exceed what is spent this year.
* **UKnight funds cannot offset the operating budget.** If the school raises $140,000 and spends $20,000 this year, the $120,000 timing difference cannot be used to cover an operating loss. Kris summarized it as: the operating budget is where we live, and the fundraising budget is where we drink.
* Kris had asked that budget figures be left out of this month's report because they are not correct. Her message to the Board: it is a work in progress, the problem is known, and it is being addressed.

**Designated Gifts**

* Jim [attribution likely] asked why some of the fundraising money could not go into the operating budget. Kris explained that the UKnight appeal used specific language (for example, technology) that restricts how those funds may be used. There is some flexibility: operating expenses that genuinely fall within the stated purposes may be charged to the fund, as a church may count a designated missions gift against its own missions spending. Restricted funds cannot be used for teacher salaries or other purposes outside the designation, and the fund balance must stay positive. Jacky Donate will have input.
* Terey said that for the **February** appeal, the school is looking at the wording so that it is careful and allows more flexibility, while still naming what is being raised for, since that is what motivates parents.

**Tuition Context**

* It was noted that tuition rose about **16%** this year (kindergarten and up). Kris and Terey explained this was a calculated increase taking Step Up into account, not an across-the-board increase; families still pay less because they qualify for Step Up, and preschool was not raised 16%. Next year's increase is 5%.

**Longer-term Planning**

* Kris hopes the school will build an **endowment**, with the interest supporting the general fund, since tuition alone is not usually enough to sustain a school's operating budget.
* Kris also wants to work with Ana to review the school's **programs** and confirm each produces a positive net result rather than a loss.
* **Budget calendar.** Kris said the Board never formally revised the budget after actual enrollment came in. She proposed a set schedule: the budget completed by **August 1** [as stated; see notes] to set tuition, then a re-budget based on enrollment numbers by a set date. Terey agreed the school needs to find a rhythm, plan the following year much earlier, and plan on a **multi-year** basis.
* Kris asked that someone else take the lead on the visionary side of the strategic plan, as her strength is the accounting, procedures, and policies. Terey asked that the admin team be part of that work, with finance helping work out how to pay for the plans.

**8:03pm - Upper School Update - Lisa [surname to confirm], Upper School Principal**

* **Enrollment.** The sixth grade has **10 students**: eight returning and two new from the community. Last year's fifth grade had about 35 students across three sections. Many families had already committed elsewhere before the middle school was announced in November.
* **Retention.** Lisa reported that families habitually treat fifth grade as the end of elementary and begin looking elsewhere. She held a coffee chat with fifth-grade parents to make the case for staying. Her hope is for two sections each of sixth and seventh grade; realistically, one of each is expected. Current students and parents report that they love the program.
* **Fifth grade pipeline.** Terey noted there are three fifth-grade sections this year, one a small Spanish immersion class of six. Next year all three fifth-grade sections will be healthy in size, and she expects upper grades to be strong in about two years.
* **Vision.** Lisa's goal is that students finishing eighth grade are "marketable." The program rests on engagement, relationships, and rigor. Fifth graders now share the upper wing, and sixth graders serve as big brothers and sisters to them.
* **Service learning.** Each class proposes and votes on a service project to carry for three years. The sixth grade chose **Paws 4 You** [spelling to confirm], an organization serving animals in need. Students handle the contact, planning, and fundraising themselves.
* **Competition and identity.** Lisa wants students competing to raise the school's profile, and aspires to a waiting list for the upper school. She and Terey see **communication** as the school's distinguishing strength.
* **Classrooms.** Flexible furniture (adjustable crescent and group tables, varied seating) lets students move and work in groups, and is working well. Lisa teaches honors life science; Mrs. Arias teaches English and Miss Jones teaches math for fifth and sixth grade. No new teachers were hired beyond Lisa.
* Terey reminded the Board of the mission statement ("motivated to think, prepared to learn, equipped to serve") and that the Board must hold the school accountable to it. She expressed the school's gratitude for Lisa joining the leadership team.
