**FCIS Accreditation Report**

* KCS received a **recommendation for continued accreditation**. The official certificate will be issued after the SCIS/FCIS board meets in April. Terey confirmed this timeline today with the accrediting body.

* **Commendations:**
  * The school's mission is clearly exhibited in students, families, and staff. Students are "grafted in truth, demonstrate leadership from a young age, and carry out the mission beyond the classroom to their homes and community."
  * Classrooms described as **engaging, structured, and caring**. Students are confident, respectful, and comfortable asking questions.
  * **Spanish immersion** highlighted as a major strength with clear long-term results.
  * **School culture is exceptional** with a strong sense of joy, belonging, and diversity across both campuses.
  * The school is in a **very strong financial position** with no debt and a consistent history of financial stability.
  * The visiting team stated: *"It is evident that the Holy Spirit is here."* They noted that while all schools paint and fix things before a visit, *"you cannot fake culture"* in a one- or two-day visit.
  * Parents randomly selected for conversation across campus all expressed strong love for the school.
  * The SCIS representative told Terey: *"Put the pedal to the metal. You are in a momentum of growth and you need to hang on and go for it. The train has left the station. If you do not take advantage of this moment, you're going to lose it."*

* **Key Recommendation - Capital Campaign Pause:**
  * The entire accreditation team recommended that KCS **not launch a capital campaign yet**. The school needs to build annual giving to a consistent and higher level first. Schools of KCS's size typically raise **8-10% of their annual budget** through annual giving (approximately $600,000-$800,000). The school has work to do to reach that level.

* **Church/School Relationship:**
  * The SCIS representative spoke at length with Pastor Keller and recommended a **"kingdom mentality"** in how the church approaches the school, meaning the church should see the school as a ministry and treat it accordingly. This was communicated to both the Board and the Session.
  * The prior accreditation concern about what happens to the school if the church ceases to function was **not elevated as a major issue** this time, given the church's improved position. However, the relationship remains a topic for continued development.

* **Return Visits:** Because the school is launching the middle school (change of program), the accreditation team will **visit annually for the next three years**. These will not be full accreditation visits, but do create accountability pressure.

* **Response Timeline:** Terey will receive formal recommendations from the FCIS board in April, along with a form requiring responses. She had held off responding until meeting with the Board tonight, and the accrediting body confirmed this timing is appropriate.

**Governance - Minimally Meets**

* Governance was the primary area where the school received a **"Minimally Meets"** rating (the lowest passing rating on a three-tier scale). Doug clarified for the Board that this is essentially a C grade.

* **Specific findings:**
  * **Standard 2.2 - Bylaws:** The team found the Board does not appear to have reviewed its bylaws recently. School policies differ from the bylaws (tuition discounts cited as an example).
  * **Board Development:** Three board members have attended SCIS governance training, which was commended, but more training and board expansion are needed.
  * **Fundraising Oversight:** The Board does not currently play a role in fundraising or provide strategic oversight of fundraising initiatives. Doug noted this was not something he would have identified as a Board responsibility, but acknowledged the accreditation standard is clear.
  * **Strategic Plan:** Must align with a multiyear financial plan.
  * **Succession Planning:** A written succession plan is needed for both board leadership and the Head of School. Kris noted a verbal plan exists but must be formalized in writing.
  * **Development Committee:** The report recommends creating a development committee on the Board to support the strategic plan.
  * **Church/School Working Structure:** Clear policies and procedures needed to sustain the relationship.

* **KCS Constitution Discovery:**

    * Board of Trustees shall consist of **at least seven members**.
    * Members serve **three-year terms**, renewable for a maximum of **three consecutive terms**.
    * The Board has authority to **appoint committees** to implement its duties.
    * Duties include supervising programs, finances, property, capital, and personnel.

  * Terey will send both the Constitution and the Board Handbook to all Board members digitally for review.

  * The Board discussed using **advisory committees** (non-board members) as a way to expand capacity, vet potential board candidates, and address specific needs. The Constitution explicitly authorizes this. Each committee would have a board member as point of contact.

**Summer Facility Usage - Mr. Jerry Zeidler**

**Genesis Program Presentation**

* KCS will **not operate a summer camp** this year for the first time in approximately eight years. The Session heard a presentation from **Genesis**, an organization that helps foster children aging out of the system transition into independent life. The connection came through the Drake and Panos families at the church.

* **Background on Genesis:**
  * Founded by a woman who lost a child in foster care in Chicago and moved to Miami to start the organization.
  * In Miami-Dade County, **240 foster children age out** of the system each year at age 18 and lose foster benefits, health benefits, and support. Of those:
    * **40%** end up homeless
    * **40%** are diagnosed with mental health concerns
    * **50%** never graduate high school
    * **3%** graduate college
    * **25%** end up in the prison system within two years
  * Genesis operates an **eight-week summer program** focused on life skills, serving teenagers aged 15-17. They currently operate in the Homestead area with capacity for about 80, and turned people away last year.

* **The Request:** Genesis asked to use the church/school campus from approximately **June 8 through early August**, needing five classrooms and the cafeteria for up to 100-120 teenagers.

* **Board Concerns:**
  * The **cafeteria is under renovation** this summer using UKnight annual fund proceeds.
  * **Vacation Bible School (VBS)** is scheduled during the same period, run by an outside church. VBS serves children through fifth grade. Mixing at-risk teenagers with young children on the same campus presents serious **safety and liability concerns**.
  * **Wear and tear:** Terey shared that a similar arrangement at a previous school approximately eight years ago resulted in significant damage and out-of-pocket costs. Similar issues have occurred at the Wayside campus with an outside organization where inadequate supervision led to property damage.
  * The school carries approximately **90% of shared facility expenses**, so summer damage costs would fall on KCS.
  * Summer maintenance and campus preparation for the new school year are already compressed into a short window.
  * The primary concern is the **safety of KCS students** and young children who may be on campus during the same period.

* **Motion - Genesis Summer Program:**
  * Jerry proposed that the Board **strongly recommend against** having Genesis utilize the church/school property this summer.
  * Kris seconded.
  * **Passed unanimously.**

* **Alternative Suggestions:**
  * The Board recommended introducing Genesis to the **Wayside campus** staff to explore whether a relationship with an appropriate age group could work at that facility.
  * **Miami Hope**, located nearby, was suggested as a potential alternative facility.
  * The Board expressed interest in finding opportunities to support and engage with Genesis **during the school year** in ways that align with KCS's mission. The school already conducts regular community outreach (food pantry partnerships, toiletries for the homeless, and other monthly service projects).

* The Board acknowledged the heartbreaking statistics and expressed genuine compassion for foster children aging out of the system, while maintaining that the Board's primary responsibility is the safety and mission of KCS.

**Sewer Project Update - Alfie Hernandez**

* Alfie reported on a meeting held earlier today with the engineer. Also present were Bill, Alice, Terey, and a representative from the construction team.
* The engineer presented **alternative options** for the water/sewer connection. Three options were initially considered, narrowed to two, which the engineer will refine:
  1. **Easement to the county:** Deed an easement for the sewer line to Miami-Dade County, making the county responsible for maintenance, testing (every ten years), and repairs.
  2. **Private line:** Keep the sewer connection entirely private, running to the property line. The school/church would be responsible for all maintenance.
  3. **Hybrid (preferred direction):** A partial easement along the property line going out to 110th, with a private section behind the school (the green area behind the building). This combines county maintenance for the main run with private control on campus.
* A **survey** is expected next week. Once received, the engineer will finalize the updated plans within approximately one week, at which point the project can go out for **permitting and bidding** to determine actual costs.
* The plans will be **presented to the Session** for a decision on which option to pursue.
