## 5. Overview of Duties

Refer to the **OCPC Deacon Duties** document (circulated in advance) for the full written reference. The chair walked through the highlights for the new deacons.

### A. Sunday Service: Deacon of the Day (DoD)

- Each Sunday has an assigned DoD. Assignments are posted in the WhatsApp group; swap in the chat if you have a conflict.
- Arrive around 10:00 AM. Stationed at the welcome desk to answer visitor questions, hand out information cards and welcome bags.
- DoD serves as the diaconate liaison with the 9:21 team via a per-Sunday WhatsApp chat that the team leader sets up.[^921]
- After service: open all interior and exterior narthex doors during the hymn following the sermon. Before leaving, turn off both AC units. Walkthroughs will be scheduled for new deacons to see where the AC units and access keys are located.

[^921]: The 9:21 team takes its name from **1 Chronicles 9:21** (ESV): "Zechariah the son of Meshelemiah was gatekeeper at the entrance of the tent of meeting." The surrounding passage (1 Chr. 9:17–27) describes the Levitical gatekeepers appointed by David and Samuel to keep watch over the gates of the house of the LORD.

### B. Sunday Service: Ushers

- Once-a-month rotation; assignments posted with the DoD schedule.
- Stationed at sanctuary doors and inside to help seat attendees.
- Keep doors closed during prayer, scripture readings, confession of faith, and responsive readings; ask anyone arriving in those moments to wait politely.
- Help seat latecomers, especially toward the front. Set up extra chairs from under the narthex stairs if needed. Per Dina, save room in the back for mothers with strollers.
- **Parking lot coverage.** One usher should be in the parking lot to assist the security guards, especially with filling the southwest grassy lot. Without an usher, both guards must leave the front of the church to manage parking, which the 9:21 team wants to avoid. Once the lot is filled and blocked off, the usher's parking duty is essentially done.

### C. Special Events

- Spring picnic (week before or after Easter)
- Reformation Sunday picnic (end of October)
- Christmas concert (first Saturday of December, typical)
- Christmas Eve services (usually two)
- Fall Kickoff Breakfast (end of summer)
- Mulching workday (one morning in November, typical)
- New Members Lunch (after each new members class; deacons attend to fellowship)
- **Turkey Drive / Thanksgiving distribution.** Wally Allen organizes; serves roughly 500+ families across Southwest Miami-Dade. Help is needed unloading the truck and distributing the weekend before Thanksgiving. Details closer to the date.

### D. Shepherding Groups

- Each shepherding group is led by an elder, partnered with a deacon (and typically a woman in the church as well).
- The elder is the primary point of contact for the group; the deacon supports as the elder directs. Practice varies widely by elder.
- **Mike Tunez** urged new deacons to be proactive: many real needs go unspoken until someone actually calls or visits. **Chris Lilburn** added that texting first (before calling) often helps, since many people will not answer an unknown number.
- An updated elder/deacon/group pairing list will be distributed by Dina in the coming weeks.

### E. Other Serving Opportunities

- Ad-hoc needs are surfaced in the WhatsApp group (rides, moves, etc.). Recent example: a few deacons helped a woman swap out a bunk bed the previous day.
- Standing committees: Facilities, Mercy Ministry, CarePortal, Hurricane Preparedness. Each has a lead deacon who reports updates at the monthly meeting.

### F. Communications

- All routine communication goes through the WhatsApp group.
- Agendas and minutes are distributed by email ahead of each meeting.

## 8. Ministry Coordinator Updates

### A. Facilities: Tyrone Shire (outgoing lead) / Bryon Snyder (incoming)

Tyrone has been spearheading facilities and is handing the ministry lead off to Bryon, who recently took on the staff role of Director of Facilities for the church. Tyrone will continue to assist Bryon as a "sidekick."

**Sanctuary roof beams.** The exterior wooden beams on the sanctuary have significant rot. Earlier this year a beam-end piece fell off the building, triggering an inspection.

- Root cause: each beam end has a concrete cap, and most were never drilled with a drainage hole, so water has been pooling for years.
- Engaged **Fourth Generation Roofers** (Robert Nielsen, whose father did work on this church long ago). On inspection, roughly **95% of the beam ends were found to be bad**; most either fell off or were removed once work began.
- Engineering review underway: **Ibrahim Sakab** (OCPC elder and engineer) jumped in early. **Doug Timmons**, an engineer who worked on the Crossbridge Church rebuild, has also been engaged and has provided drawings and advice.
- Currently working on permits and soliciting three contractor quotes. Scope may include gutter replacement, which would push the cost higher. The project will need **session approval** given the cost.
- Ibrahim is running weekly Zoom meetings to keep stakeholders aligned on progress.

**AC project.**

- **Phase 1 complete:** cooling tower installation, led by Jonathan, after last summer's AC problems in the sanctuary. Running well so far.
- **Phase 2 planned:** full replacement of the downstairs AC units. Another large-ticket item that will need session approval.

- **Steve Hendrikse** previously led this effort. He rolled off the diaconate but remains willing to assist.

### E. Mercy Aid Procedure Clarification (in response to question from Joe Sirgany)
