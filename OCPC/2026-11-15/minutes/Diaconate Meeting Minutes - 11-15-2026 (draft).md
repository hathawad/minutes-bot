## 3. Recap of Previous Events

### A. Reformation Sunday Picnic (October 25)

The picnic went well despite rain, which the deacons had expected and adjusted for. The following week's weather was far worse, so the timing worked out.

**Harold Hall.** Because of the weather, tables were also set up inside Harold Hall, and attendees appreciated having an air-conditioned place to sit, especially older members. Going forward, Harold Hall will be set up in addition to the outdoor seating.

**Event tent idea.** Tyrone Shire shared photos of a large, open-sided event tent he saw at an outdoor event the day before, long enough to hold several rows of round tables of eight. A setup like this could protect future picnics from weather and accommodate church growth. Others noted that large tents of this size are typically regulated and must be professionally installed, so renting is likely the better option than buying. Smaller modular tents that link together are a cheaper alternative. The chair suggested revisiting the idea closer to the spring picnic.

**Hospitality toward the Spanish-language congregation.** Members of the Spanish-language congregation that meets at the church were present during the picnic but had not been invited or planned for in the meal count, which made for an awkward moment. Deacons agreed it would be good hospitality and a chance to build unity to include them at future picnics. This needs to be planned in advance: coordinate with their leadership, get a headcount for meals, and invite them to help. Deacons also suggested that, as people exit the sanctuary after the service, deacons help direct them toward the food lines and seating so everyone knows where to go. The chair will raise both points ahead of the next picnic.

### B. New Members Lunch (November 8)

Setup, serving, and food changeover went smoothly. More people attended than expected, and the chair thanked the deacons who brought out additional tables and chairs.

### C. Mulch Day (November 14)

The chair thanked everyone who came, including deacons who brought their sons and spouses.

- **Mulch.** The free mulch did not work out (the load available contained palm material; hardwood such as oak is needed). Bulk mulch was ordered from a wholesale supplier instead and dumped in a pile in the senior parking area, for about **$675** ($500 for the mulch and $175 for delivery), significantly less than the bagged mulch bought last year.
- **Timing.** The delivery was scheduled for 10:00 but arrived close to noon. The group used the wait to clean up the courtyard, which looks much improved. Rossi Siewnarine cooked hot dogs for lunch.
- **Pile vs. bags.** Those present agreed the pile was faster: with a front loader and wheelbarrows, spreading took about an hour, and no bags had to be laid out early, cut open, and emptied. With more people, more wheelbarrows would be needed to avoid a bottleneck.
- **Coverage.** About half the usual area was covered, since only one truckload arrived. The courtyard and the main stretch were done. The bed in front of the sanctuary was left alone at Trudy's request. With the savings, Bryon Snyder will buy additional bags for her area and for touch-ups around the property, which he will do over time. Deacons will be told if more help is needed.
- **Next year.** Schedule the bulk delivery in advance (not the day of), ideally as two piles: one in the parking spaces on the right side and one where this year's pile was.

## 4. Upcoming Events

### A. Thanksgiving Meal and Service (Wednesday, November 18)

No diaconate setup or takedown is expected; the elders will handle most of the serving. Deacons are encouraged to attend.

### B. Turkey Drive (Friday, November 20 to Saturday, November 21)

Wally Allen reported that the turkeys have arrived and are being held at the supplier. He is confirming the numbers today. Some items have already been picked up, and the rest will be picked up this week.

- **Friday morning:** Wally will be at the supplier from about 6:00 AM. The truck, carrying rice, potatoes, and other items, is expected back at the church around 1:00 to 1:30 PM. Help is needed to unload. Wally will post in the WhatsApp group when the truck is on its way back. A deacon offered to send his sons after school.
- **Friday afternoon:** Unloaded items will be sorted and laid out on the tables before packing. A deacon suggested combining unloading and sorting by placing items directly where they go, and Wally agreed.
- **Friday 6:00 PM:** Doors open for packing. About 40 volunteers walk the line filling bags. Doors will not open before 6:00, so sorting is finished without a crowd. Deacons are needed during packing to keep the tables replenished and for quality control.
- **Saturday morning:** Distribution is planned to start at 8:00 AM, depending on how early the turkeys can be picked up; Wally will confirm the earliest time in the next few days. The most help is needed at the start, loading the turkeys onto the truck and bringing them in with hand trucks. Students from a local high school will help load cars and trucks, and that part should be finished by about 9:00 AM. Deacons who can only help early are welcome to leave after loading.
- **Delivery sites:** The drivers who normally deliver to the three sites have it covered. Distribution should wrap up around 2:00 PM.
- **Service hours:** Asked whether students can earn community service hours, Wally said no; the turkey drive is a chance to serve.

### C. Christmas Concerts (Saturday, December 5)

Per the agenda: three shows, 600 tickets per show with room for those without tickets; the 2:30 show is free and the 5:00 and 7:30 shows are $10 per ticket. The chair confirmed the schedule with Dina. Each show runs about an hour and fifteen minutes:

- 2:30 to 3:45 PM
- 5:00 to 6:15 PM
- 7:30 to 8:45 PM

**Staffing.** Several deacons will be singing in the choir, running sound, or serving in other roles, so the chair asked for input on arrival times and shifts: one shift per concert, or two shifts overlapping at the middle show. A deacon noted that parking volunteers need to arrive at least an hour before a show, and that the turnover between shows, with people leaving and arriving at once, is the hardest part. Some deacons plan to attend one show and work the others.

**Equipment.**
- **Radios:** Everyone working parking should have a radio, so volunteers in different lots (including across the street) can call out open spots.
- **Flashlights:** Red-light flashlights for parking volunteers. The chair will check whether more need to be ordered.
- **Vests:** The vests in storage downstairs are the breathable kind and are fine to use, unlike last year's heavy vinyl ones.
- The chair has a call this week with Dina, Diego, and Robert about the concert and will raise radios and flashlights.

**Golf carts.** Two six-seat golf carts have been ordered to shuttle people before and after each show. Mark may bring an additional cart; drivers are still to be determined.

**Traffic and police.** Two police officers will help with traffic at the entrances. The two lanes by the banyan tree will be blocked off, so the only ways in will be the 144th Street entrance and the main entrance, with an officer at each. Diego runs parking for large True North concerts and has a system that works well.

**Lighting and safety.** Additional parking lot lighting is not needed for this event, though it could be part of the master plan. Volunteers will carry flashlights. A large light display (with snow) is planned out front, so volunteers must make sure no one drives through the circle while children are there.

**Parking areas.** A deacon asked whether a new parking map is needed for the concerts. The chair will confirm the parking areas this week; overflow will likely be across the street or along Old Cutler between the bike path and the edge of the property. For scale, about 700 people attended last week's baptism service (sanctuary capacity is roughly 750), and about 20 spaces on the youth side were still open, with no one parked on the youth building grass, so parking should be sufficient. The 2:30 and 5:00 shows are already sold out.

**Performers.** Dina has asked all performers (choir and musicians) to park on the youth building grass, leaving the parking spaces for concertgoers. Musicians will arrive around 12:30 PM and leave around 9:30 PM. Deacons could use the golf carts to shuttle musicians and help carry heavy equipment; performers typically drop off equipment at the door and then go park.

**Inviting the Spanish-language congregation.** In the spirit of the picnic discussion, a deacon suggested letting the Spanish-language congregation know about the concerts in case they would like to attend. Outreach to them about Thanksgiving has already happened.

**Seating and walk-ups.**
- **Overflow chairs:** The count after last month's meeting found room for about 70 additional chairs in the back of the sanctuary. Dina has approved setting them out beforehand, so 70 to 90 chairs (from under the stairs) will be in place before the first show rather than pulled in last minute. Ushers should keep an eye on the arrangement as people move chairs around.
- **Couches:** The couches will be moved against the wall by the window to clear the bottleneck between the desk and the couch and keep the exit path open.
- **Doors:** The north side doors (east and west) will be locked to the public. Musicians and performers will be going in and out through them all day with heavy equipment, so deacons should expect that; the general public should not enter that way.
- **Walk-up tickets:** A block of tickets will be held for each show and sold at the door to people who arrive without one. The goal is that no one is turned away unless the sanctuary is at capacity.
- **At capacity:** No separate waiting area will be opened, to keep the building secure (the downstairs area is reserved for the musicians). Guests who cannot get in can wait in their cars or come back for a later show, and deacons should offer those options courteously rather than a flat no.

**Roles.** The diaconate's role is parking and ushering. Ticket and cash transactions will be handled by Dina, Ray, or whoever is running the ticket table; deacons will step in only if asked.

**Shifts.** After discussing one shift per show versus overlapping shifts, the deacons agreed on **two four-hour shifts: 1:30 to 5:30 PM and 5:30 PM to close.** Deacons should arrive about an hour before a show (a line forms early) and stay about 30 minutes after. The handoff falls about 30 minutes into the 5:00 show, when nearly everyone is seated; the turnover between shows, with people leaving and arriving at once, is the worst time to switch. Deacons who can only help for part of a shift are encouraged to come when they can.

- **After the last show:** Help is needed to strike the stage equipment and put the chairs away. With enough people it takes about 15 to 20 minutes.
- **Sign-ups:** The chair will post both shifts on WhatsApp to get a headcount. Volunteers from the new parking and ushering ministry are also expected to help and can learn on the job.

**Ushering.** Ticket scanning should make arrivals more orderly. A deacon suggested stationing ushers at the front to fill the sanctuary from the front row back, one row at a time, which seats people much faster. Ushers should also help people move in from the ends of the rows. Guests using wheelchairs often sit at the row ends, and many people prefer an aisle seat, so ushers should ask politely and accommodate anyone with a reason to stay on the aisle. Larger groups hoping to sit together will need some help as well.
