## 3. Resource Directory

Pastor Greg Foss has compiled a community resource directory (seven to eight pages) to help deacons and elders assist people whose needs fall outside what the Mercy Ministry can provide: shelter for the night, hospitals, social services, food, pregnancy resources, and similar. Each entry includes a clickable link to the provider's website, along with suggestions for how deacons can sit with and help people. He also highlighted **2-1-1**, a hotline that connects callers with more than 4,000 resources in Miami.

- The directory was attached to the agenda email. Pastor Greg will make hard copies for the deacons and email it again.
- The chair suggested keeping a hard copy at the welcome desk. The directory has been **posted and pinned in the deacons' WhatsApp group.**

**Guidance on use.** In response to a question about how to help someone standing in front of you, Pastor Greg explained that the directory is mainly for last-minute situations; many needs can be handled through the Mercy Ministry. Where someone has no resources at all, a deacon can make the call on their behalf (for example, to a homeless shelter). He cautioned against giving rides to people you don't know or handing out checks without follow-up. Mercy requests involve getting to know the person by email and phone, inviting them to church, and following up, including through the evangelism team. Deacons facing a difficult situation should contact Pastor Greg or Mike Tunez.

## 4. Ushering and Parking Ministry

**Background.** The chair, Mike Tunez, Pastor Mike, and Pastor Greg met this week to begin forming ushering and parking ministries that would let church members who are not deacons help. The effort is in its early stages with no firm timeline; the goal is to have additional hands by the Christmas concerts. The chair has asked several deacons to help train new volunteers, and thanked them.

**Why now.** Pastor Greg explained that regular attendance is now around 620 to 630. Moving to two services would require regular attendance of about 700; when that happens, the two services would be identical rather than one traditional and one contemporary. Growth, the capital campaign, and the hoped-for new parking lot are all reasons to build out parking and ushering now. Rick Sanda described visiting a large church where volunteers greeted him from the parking lot to the door and walked him to his class, so that a visitor was never left alone.

**Structure.**
- The ministries would fall under the diaconate's purview but would not be limited to deacons. Women, including deacons' wives and the women's ministry, are welcome to serve as ushers and in parking.
- Training would be provided for both parking and ushering. Research is needed on how other churches organize this.
- Asked whether this would replace or overlap with the Welcome Ministry, Pastor Greg said the two would likely work hand in hand (greeters are stationary while ushers move around), and Ray would need to be included in that conversation. Several deacons noted that ushering already involves a great deal of greeting.
- Ushers will continue to be asked to help people move in to make room in the pews, which can be difficult but grows more important as attendance increases.

**Usher name tags.** The current usher tags do not carry names. The group agreed to ask Ray about name tags for ushers; there are about 28 deacons and only five or six ushers are needed each week. Congregation-wide name tags were discussed briefly but have not been supported by staff in the past.

**Feedback.** The chair relayed that staff said the deacons are doing a good job with ushering, and thanked everyone who has been helping.

## 5. Upcoming Events

### A. Reformation Sunday Picnic: Sunday, October 25

**Arrive 8:30 AM.** Deacons with other commitments that morning are understood; otherwise, the more hands the better.

**Tents.** Anyone with large pop-up tents is asked to bring them, for the condiment and food areas rather than all of the seating. A concern was raised about anchoring tents in wind; whether the church has sandbags is **TBD**.

**Parking.** Per the parking diagram and instructions in the chair's email, which came from church security, certain entrances will be blocked to keep the grounds secure. Rather than parking across the street as in past years, deacons will use their cars to block the grassy lot in the southwest corner wherever the berm is not high enough to stop a vehicle, the 146th Street entrance, and the lot exit onto the side street (marked with red dashes on the diagram). Senior parking will be kept on the other side. Deacons will be guided to their spots on arrival and can move cars as needed. The 9:21 team will also arrive early.

**Food and setup.** Sports Grill will handle the grilling. Deacons are needed for the beans and coleslaw, with Wally Allen as the go-between with Sports Grill, and for setting up tables and chairs. If rain threatens, bring plastic table covers and towels, and prop chairs up to keep water from pooling, as was done at a past picnic. The deacons who sing in the choir will help with setup and step away only to sing. The chair will ask whether some of the regular helpers can cover ushering so the deacons can focus on setup and food.

**Safety.** Deacons were reminded to stay alert during the event:
- At a past event, young children were seen going to and sitting in parked cars on the perimeter, near traffic, without their parents. The group discussed asking parents to keep children out of the vehicles.
- At another past event, an unfamiliar adult approached children and spoke to them in a way that made them uncomfortable, then left before it could be addressed.
- Deacons do not need to be on the Welcome team to notice unfamiliar faces. If something seems off, check in with the children or the person, or alert the 9:21 team. It may be innocent, but it never hurts.

**Seating.** A deacon passed along feedback from a church member who no longer attends the picnics because there are not enough places to sit. Deacons were encouraged to invite people to the picnic. Ideas discussed:
- Make clear that tables are reserved for elderly members, guests, and visitors, and encourage regulars to bring camping chairs and picnic blankets.
- Bring out rectangular tables in addition to the round tables.
- Ask Dina for an expected headcount from the sign-up; if it is higher than usual, bring out more tables.
- Space may be tighter this year if rides or bounce houses take up part of the grounds; one or two are expected.

### F. Christmas Concerts: Saturday, December 5

This is Old Cutler's **50th Christmas concert**. The format announced to the diaconate:

- **Three shows: 2:30, 5:00, and 7:30 PM** (adding a 2:30 show to the usual two). The hope is that this eases the crowding and frustration from last year.
- **600 tickets per show.** The 2:30 show is free; the 5:00 and 7:30 shows are **$10 per ticket**.
- Each show leaves room for people without tickets, seated first come, first served, since many regular attendees have never needed a ticket.
- The elders discussed the format at length; charging for the later shows was the compromise reached. The fee is meant to discourage people from reserving many tickets and using only a few.
- Payment is expected to be almost entirely electronic, with a QR code as the ticket. No cash sales are planned, and the shows will likely sell out in advance.
- It is a long day for the orchestra and choir as well, and they will need to be fed.

**Overflow seating.** The chair asked for a couple of volunteers after today's service to estimate how many additional chairs can fit in the back of the sanctuary, filling as many seats as is safe while keeping exits clear.

**Staffing.** In addition to ushering and parking, tickets will need to be checked at the doors, which adds to the manpower need. This is further motivation to have the new ushering and parking ministry running by December. Welcome volunteers for the day are coordinated through Ray and the front desk. The chair will post WhatsApp events for each show; deacons should sign up for as many as they can. Deacons performing in the concert are not expected to usher.
