### B. Mulch Day: Saturday, November 14

**Date set: Saturday, November 14.** The last Saturday of October (October 31) was ruled out as too close to the picnic, and Bryon Snyder is on PTO the first Saturday of November.

**Free mulch.** Armando Camejo has a contact at Big John's Tree Service, which gives away the mulch it produces from downed trees. It will be brown, not orange, and delivered loose by the truckload rather than in bags. Armando will see the contact this week and coordinate delivery. Bryon Snyder will designate a drop spot on campus so deliveries can begin ahead of the day.

**Equipment.** Because the mulch will arrive loose, wheelbarrows and rakes are needed. The church has a few, and deacons are asked to bring their own. If anyone has access to a Bobcat or similar small loader, it would allow mulch to be dropped directly in each area rather than hauled from one pile.

**Scope.** Mulch Day is a general grounds cleanup, including trimming bushes and hedges in the courtyard, not only mulching. Last year the group finished by 11:00 AM, well ahead of prior years, largely because several deacons who could not stay came early and completed the trimming before the 9:00 AM start. The chair encouraged anyone who can come early to do so. This is an all-hands event, but no one should cancel travel plans for it.

### E. Thanksgiving Food Basket and Turkey Drive: Friday, November 20 to Saturday, November 21

Wally Allen, who has led the drive for many years (the church has run it for 15 to 20 years), described this year's plan. Last year the drive served **550 families** and still had to turn people away; this year the goal is **600**. Wally expressed his thanks for the number of younger men now in the diaconate, since the drive depends on physical help.

- **Friday, November 20, about 3:00 PM (heaviest need).** The delivery truck arrives around 3:00 PM. Volunteers unload all the cans, packages, and turkeys (heavy lifting) and set up tables so the room is ready when church families arrive at **6:00 PM** to pack the baskets. Packing typically takes about two hours. The doors stay closed until 6:00 so everyone can take part.
- **Saturday, November 21, 8:00 AM.** Baskets are then loaded onto trucks by a line of volunteers, including students from local schools, for delivery to distribution sites. More and more recipient organizations now pick up at the church directly, so roughly three quarters of the work is done on campus. A truck and a few drivers are needed to deliver to the remaining sites (about five). Distribution should wrap up around 2:00 to 3:00 PM.
- **Sign-up table.** Beginning the last Sunday of October, a table will be set up in the front for three Sundays, staffed before and after services, to take donations (most giving is online) and sign up Friday volunteers. The chair will recruit table volunteers through the WhatsApp group.

Times will be confirmed closer to the date.

### F. Christmas Concerts: Saturday, December 5

The chair shared what he has heard from Dina so far:

- **Schedule.** The two concerts have typically run at about 5:00 and 7:00 PM. This year an **extra thirty minutes** will be added between shows so the first audience can clear the parking lot before the second arrives.
- **Tickets.** Tickets will be required, with **750 per show**, effectively a full house. Tickets are expected to be free. The purpose is to spread attendance across the shows after last year's crowds exceeded capacity. How people without tickets will be handled (a waitlist or first-come standby line) has not been decided.
- **Possible third show.** Dina raised the possibility of adding a third performance, either Friday evening or earlier on Saturday. The group noted a decision deadline is needed given the demands on the musicians.
- **Davis Chapel overflow room.** Regardless of the ticketing and show decisions, Dina wants to improve the overflow experience in Davis Chapel beyond last year's single TV, likely with a larger screen or projector and better audio. Deacons with technical background were asked to think about what would need to be rented and how to set up the room. Renting was felt to be likely cheaper than purchasing.

**Planning meeting.** A logistics meeting will be scheduled in the next couple of weeks with Dina, Sean, Bryon Snyder, and the 9:21 team liaison. The chair will attend and **invite one or two additional deacons**, particularly those who worked the parking lot last year, to bring observations on what could be done better.

**Discussion.**

- **Crowd control and capacity.** Last year the doors had to be closed once the sanctuary was full, which led to tense exchanges with people who were turned away, some of whom had family already inside. It was noted that attendance at the doors has not been counted in past years, and that counting will be important given the occupancy limit. These details are expected to be worked out at the planning meeting.
- **Communicating the ticket requirement.** Concern was raised that longtime attendees from the community are used to simply showing up. Suggestions included the church website, flyers, service announcements, signage out front, and possibly a PSA on Christian radio. Ticket distribution is assumed to use some form of QR code.
- **Parking team equipment.** It was suggested the parking team get inexpensive flashlights with red (airline-style) wands so drivers can see them after dark, and a few more breathable safety vests. The chair agreed.
- **Golf cart shuttles.** As in past years, multiple golf carts will shuttle attendees from the overflow parking across the street and elsewhere on the lot. A member of the congregation has typically brought his own six-seat cart and provided a second. One of the carts broke down last year.
- **Marking grass parking.** It was suggested the parking spaces in the grass lots be marked with spray paint so drivers can see them, which should increase capacity and make the parking team's job easier. The chair will raise it at the planning meeting.
- **West-side access lane.** Cars parked along the west side of the church last year blocked the lane needed for emergency vehicle access, even after drivers were told not to park there. Additional cones are needed to keep that lane clear.

## 5. Transition of Duties from Session

Carried over from August 15. Adam Gaschott and Mike Tunez met with Pastor Mike to discuss what the diaconate could take off the Session's plate. Mike Tunez's primary idea had been finances.

**Finances stay with the Session.** Pastor Mike made the case that because the Session makes the big-picture decisions for the church, major financial decisions should stay with it. He has seen other churches where the Session decides something needs to be done but must then seek financial approval from a separate body, leaving the process disjointed.

**Budget Committee.** Instead, Pastor Mike suggested involving more deacons in the Budget Committee. The committee meets one or two evenings a year, typically toward the end of the year, to review the prepared budget for the following year line by line and approve it. Its membership has been a couple of elders, a couple of members at large, and two deacons (including the chair). **Deacons interested in joining should tell the chair**, who will pass names to Dina.

**Facilities Committee.** Bryon Snyder, as facilities manager, now leads facilities, with Tyrone Shire assisting (Tyrone previously represented the diaconate side of facilities). Deacons are invited to join them as a facilities committee. Bryon remains the point person for all major facilities issues and will delegate as needed. Bryon noted that the upcoming capital campaign and building upgrades will take "a small village" to navigate, and that he will need help once that work begins, though it is still some way off. The ask is mostly "see something, say something" and pitching in on time-consuming tasks, and he thanked Rick Sanda for his help with the pews. **Deacons interested in either committee should tell the chair.**

## 6. Ministry Coordinator Updates

### A. Facilities: Bryon Snyder

**Gutter and beam repair underway.** Scaffolding is up and work has begun on removing the gutters and cutting back the beam ends, which will then be capped and painted. This is the interim fix until a possible full roof replacement is evaluated. The work was designed with a structural engineer and an architect, meets code, and is approved by the city. It is expected to take about **three weeks**, and the contractor is moving quickly.

**Copper salvage.** The church negotiated a reduced price on the project in exchange for salvaged copper, rather than selling the copper separately. Remaining copper will be sold to the same buyer, with proceeds going toward several smaller projects.

**Youth building.** The downstairs renovation is complete, mostly painting and repairs on the facilities side, with interior design by Eric and his wife. Deacons who have not seen it were encouraged to take a look. **The upstairs renovation will begin in the next couple of weeks.**

**Youth building cleanout.** The chair thanked Michael Batts and Rick Sanda for hauling items from the youth building to the dump. Several large waterlogged pieces (couches and large chairs) remain, requiring three or four people and about two truckloads. The chair will schedule a time through the WhatsApp group.

### B. Mercy Ministry: Christopher Lilburn (reporting for Mike Tunez)

Mike Tunez was absent; Chris Lilburn gave the update.

The Mercy Ministry is working well. Requests come through Dina, applicants complete the forms, and each case is fully vetted. In the last month the ministry helped roughly **six or seven families, totaling about $8,000 to $10,000**. Assistance is being provided this week to a longtime member, which is expected to resolve the need for ongoing monthly support.

**Fund balance.** Although the fund is regularly drawn down, gifts replenish it quickly, and the balance has stayed in the **$30,000 to $50,000** range. Chris observed that this is more than the ministry needs to hold, and the committee is focused on directing it to those in genuine need. He noted that many people do not ask for help until they are in serious difficulty, so the ability to respond almost immediately matters.

### C. CarePortal: Christopher Lilburn

Chris Lilburn, Iain Kelso, and Mike Tunez are handling CarePortal. OCPC keeps roughly **$2,000** in its CarePortal fund at any given time. Requests come from vetted partner churches and organizations and are handled online. Chris funded several requests this week. Most requests are in the church's area, largely in Goulds and farther south, and typically run $75 to $200: groceries for a struggling single mother, a backpack, a bus pass, or school uniforms. Chris noted the number of high school students living in cars who need such basics.

### D. Mercy Ministry: Additional Notes

**Approval rule.** Chris noted that disbursements from the Mercy Fund require the agreement of all five members of the mercy committee.

**Beyond financial aid.** For one member facing significant credit card debt, the committee has brought in a church member experienced in negotiating with creditors to help work toward reducing the debt. Chris and that member planned to meet with the member receiving help after service. The chair welcomed the ministry going beyond giving money to helping people address the underlying problem.

### E. Hurricane Preparedness (Joseph Sirgany absent)

The chair gave a brief update in Joe Sirgany's absence. No storms have affected the area so far this season, and the group was asked to pray that continues through the remaining months. The list of members needing help is in hand, and deacons will assist if a storm threatens.
